# A short history of Hrafn

## 2016 to 2018: the idea

Hrafn Travel Technologies was founded in Reykjavik in September 2016 by Sigríður Hallgrímsdóttir and two former colleagues from a tour operator. They had noticed that small Icelandic tour companies were managing departures with spreadsheets and paper, and that the large global booking platforms took commissions of up to 25%. The first prototype was a booking calendar built in a weekend and sold to a single whale-watching boat for ISK 15,000 a month.

## 2019: Skerry launches

After raising ISK 120 million in a seed round from two Icelandic funds, the team of seven released Skerry, a hosted booking engine with a flat subscription instead of a commission. By the end of 2019 there were 38 operators on the platform. The name Skerry came from the small rocky islands that dot the Icelandic coast, and the raven logo was drawn by a friend of the founders on a napkin at a cafe on Laugavegur.

## 2020: the pandemic

Tourism collapsed in March 2020. Revenue fell 61% in a quarter. The board chose not to lay anyone off, partly thanks to government support schemes and partly because the founders took a pay cut of 30% for nine months. The quiet period was used to rewrite the checkout and to build the Geysir pricing engine. Guðmundur Ólafsson joined during this time.

## 2021 to 2022: recovery and growth

Bookings returned in summer 2021, and by 2022 the company had 120 operators and 28 employees. The first Nordic customers, from Norway and the Faroe Islands, signed up. A Series A of EUR 6 million in 2022 funded the expansion of the engineering team and the move to the Hólmaslóð office, which the company still occupies.

## 2023 to 2024: maturity

The platform reached profitability in 2023. In 2024 revenue reached ISK 798 million and the customer count 214. Support moved to extended hours, the hiring process was formalised and the company got its first internal knowledge base, which you are reading a part of.

## Values

We said four things on the first wall of the office and they remain: be honest with customers, make small operators look big, keep things simple, and go home at a reasonable hour.

## What next

The 2025 plan is to move to Fiskislóð, ship the mobile app and finish the database migration. Beyond that, the board has chosen to stay focused on operators rather than build a consumer marketplace.

## Anecdotes

The first Skerry customer, the whale-watching boat from 2016, is still on the platform and has outlived three laptops. In 2018 the founders could not afford a proper office and rented a corner of a design studio on Grandagarður, sharing a printer with a ceramics artist who now has a quarter of the walls in the building covered in her work. In 2021, during the first summer after the pandemic, an automated test accidentally sent 4,000 test confirmation emails to a distribution list; the incident is the reason every non-production environment now has an outbound email allowlist.

## Offices
