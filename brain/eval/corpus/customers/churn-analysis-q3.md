# Churn analysis, Q3 2024

Prepared by Elín (Head of Support) and Björn (CFO). Period: 1 July to 30 September 2024.

## Summary

Eleven operators cancelled during the quarter, versus 6 in Q2. Lost annual revenue is ISK 9.8 million, 1.2% of the annualised base. Gross logo churn for the year to date is 4.1%, within the 5% target.

## Reasons given

| Reason | Count |
|---|---|
| Business closed or sold | 4 |
| Price | 3 |
| Missing feature (multi-currency) | 2 |
| Moved to a competitor suite | 1 |
| Unknown | 1 |

## Observations

Price-driven churn was concentrated among operators with fewer than 100 bookings a year. For them our entry plan at ISK 38,000 per month is a high fixed cost. We are testing a seasonal plan, with pauses in winter months, at ISK 21,000 per month active.

The two operators who left over multi-currency are both Swedish and sell in SEK; the Q2 roadmap addresses this but does not include SEK at first. We should say so honestly in sales conversations.

## Support signals

Customers who raised more than three tickets in their first 60 days were 2.6 times more likely to churn. Common themes were calendar sync and refund handling. We recommend a proactive call on day 30 for every new account.

## Positive signals

Net revenue retention for operators over two years old is 118%. Seven operators upgraded to the larger plan in the quarter. The Hveravellir onboarding showed that guided setup halves the time to go-live.

## Recommendations

1. Launch the seasonal plan in January 2025 as a trial with 15 operators.
2. Add a day-30 success call to onboarding.
3. Publish a candid multi-currency timeline on the website.
4. Review the refund workflow in the console with the product team.

## Next review

Q4 analysis will be presented at the February management meeting.

## Appendix: method and caveats

Churn is counted when an operator formally gives notice or stops paying, whichever comes first. Seasonal operators that pause in winter are not counted as churn unless they fail to return by the following June. The reasons were collected in exit calls by support; 9 of the 11 operators agreed to an exit call, and the remaining two reasons are inferred from the cancellation form. Revenue figures use the contract value at cancellation, not what the operator actually paid in the quarter.

We compared churned operators with a matched sample of 11 retained operators of similar size and age. Retained operators logged in to the console 3.4 times more often during their first 90 days, and 8 of 11 had connected at least one channel manager. Having an integration appears to be a strong stickiness signal and onboarding should push for it.

The sample is small, so none of these differences are statistically significant on their own; the point of the analysis is to produce hypotheses that the seasonal plan trial and the day-30 call can test.
